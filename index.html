I'm drawn to resource-constrained systems because the constraints often reveal something interesting about the assumptions we take for granted.

Many design choices become conventions because they work well in a particular setting. But when the hardware, timing requirements, or threat model changes, those assumptions may no longer hold. I enjoy going back to the original problem, understanding why existing approaches look the way they do, and exploring parts of the solution space that may have been overlooked.

I'm a PhD candidate in Cybersecurity at Georgia Tech, working with Prof. J. Sukarno Mertoguno at CSAFA Lab. Much of my work asks how we can make secure and resilient computing practical on systems with limited processing resources or strict deadlines. More recently, I've also been studying efficient ML execution on FPGA and other constrained hardware.

Before Georgia Tech, I worked on compiler-based program debloating and software security at the Internet Security and Privacy Lab at LUMS.

02 / Research

Selected research

Systems security and efficient computation, often in places where timing, memory, and hardware budgets shape what is possible.

Redundancy and reselection in SubprocessBFT++ · From my research diagram (select to enlarge)

2023–2025 · BIRD Energy Consortium, Phase 1

SubprocessBFT++

Resilience mechanisms such as software replication can be difficult to deploy on robotic controllers with limited computing resources and tight scan-cycle deadlines.

SubprocessBFT++ decomposes controller software into subprocesses and uses diversified variants with supervisory wrappers. The design explores redundancy and reselection as ways to limit the effects of control-flow hijacking on single-controller systems.

The work has been evaluated through simulations, fault-injection experiments, and microcontroller-oriented studies, with attention to recovery behavior and execution overhead under the evaluated assumptions.

2025–present · BIRD Energy Consortium, Phase 2

JBODSP: making more of FPGA DSP resources

I'm investigating how DSP clusters on FPGAs can be organized as configurable computational resources for machine-learning workloads, rather than treated only as isolated arithmetic units.

The current Python architectural simulator explores 1D convolution tiling, logical compute-unit scheduling, on-chip memory limits, and idealized throughput for Intel Arria 10 configurations.

The aim is to evaluate architectural and data-movement choices before hardware implementation. Double-buffering and more effective workload distribution are design directions being investigated, not yet measured hardware speedups.

Other work in progress

Real-time resilient execution

2026–present · Systems & virtualization

Extending diversified subprocess execution to real-time cloud workloads. I'm investigating how Xen RTDS scheduling, Unikraft, execution budgets, and controlled output commitment might support resilience while respecting deadline constraints. This is ongoing design and implementation research.

LLM sparsity and error propagation

2026–present · Efficient inference

Exploring magnitude-based suppression of attention QKV parameters and how approximation errors propagate through LLM inference. The broader goal is to understand when sparse execution and specialized zero-skipping hardware can meaningfully reduce compute and memory requirements.

EviLLM and LLM interface security

LLM security · Browser instrumentation

Investigating security vulnerabilities in web-based LLM workflows through browser-level experimentation. EviLLM includes Chrome extension components for request inspection and modification, and UI/DOM instrumentation for studying how adversarial content reaches users and models.

The SubprocessBFT++ and JBODSP projects have been conducted in the context of the BIRD Energy Consortium's U.S. Department of Energy-supported research. Descriptions on this page distinguish ongoing designs and analytical prototypes from experimentally validated results.
